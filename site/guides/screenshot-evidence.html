Check a screenshot before asking AI: an evidence worksheet

Updated November 16, 2026 · Editorial guide

Practice checking labels, arithmetic and missing context with a worked example, then use a local checklist to prepare your own screenshot question.

Start with a question the image can answer

A screenshot preserves visible pixels, not the underlying spreadsheet, filters or records. Before asking an AI to explain a number, identify what you want to compare and what evidence is actually visible. This worksheet uses invented support-ticket figures. It is an editorial exercise, not a screenshot of CaptureAsk or a report of a successful AI upload.

Work out what follows from the numbers

Week 1 has 120 − 108 = 12 more opened tickets than resolved tickets. Week 2 has 135 − 132 = 3. Across these two periods, opened minus resolved is 15. That is a net difference, not proof that the final backlog is 15. The starting backlog and any other movements, such as reopened or transferred tickets, are not supplied.

A useful answer should state this boundary. If an answer says “the team finished with 15 tickets,” ask which visible evidence establishes the starting balance. If it says that resolution performance improved, check which measure it used: the counts, the ratio of resolved to opened, or a different metric. A change in a ratio is not by itself evidence of a change in staff productivity.

Keep the labels in your crop

A crop containing only 120, 108, 135 and 132 removes the category labels and period names. Include the title, units, row and column headings and any visible filter or date-range controls. Leave out unrelated account details. When the necessary context is spread across two windows, consider a full-screen capture; when one table is sufficient, a smaller capture can keep text easier to read. See the area-capture walkthrough for the difference between a visible-page selection and a long scrolling page.

Use a prompt with a stopping rule

Using only the visible table, calculate opened minus resolved for each week and for the two weeks together. Show the arithmetic. Explain what additional information would be needed to determine the final backlog. If a label is unreadable, say so instead of guessing.

Compare the answer with the table yourself. The expected differences are 12, 3 and 15. The final backlog remains undetermined. This check does not require trusting a model's confidence or accepting an explanation that sounds plausible.

Before sharing your own image

Review the actual attachment, not only the rectangle you dragged. Ensure text is readable at normal size, relevant headings remain visible and confidential information has been removed. Covering a field with a translucent shape can leave it readable; inspect the final saved image. Do not send passwords, payment details or private messages merely because they appeared beside the useful content.

Opening an AI service is a separate step from attaching an image. Look for its attachment indicator and inspect the image before submitting your message. If no attachment appears, use the saved file and the service's own upload control. The handoff troubleshooting guide distinguishes capture, navigation, sign-in and attachment failures.

A repeatable review record

  1. Write the question and identify the exact visible evidence that supports it.
  2. Note the capture date, relevant reporting period, units and filters.
  3. List information that is missing or unreadable.
  4. Remove unrelated personal information and inspect the final image.
  5. Check the answer's calculations and source references against the image.
  6. Verify consequential decisions against the original records or official guidance.

Keep this record with the result when it matters. A screenshot can help you explain an issue, but it cannot establish facts that were outside the captured view.
